# Mapping Trust and Safety in Urban Mobility from Data in the Wild

## Working Title
Mapping Trust and Safety in Urban Mobility from Data in the Wild: A Framework for Understanding Rider Experience in Autonomous and Human-Driven Mobility Services

## Research Question
How can publicly available rider narratives, sentiment indicators, and geospatial analysis be used to identify trust, comfort, safety, and operational-design issues in autonomous mobility services?

## Contribution
Traditional AV safety evaluation focuses on collisions, disengagements, and technical performance. This framework expands evaluation to include rider experience, trust, emotional comfort, pickup and dropoff quality, and perceived safety.

## Methods
- Scenario classification
- Sentiment analysis
- Geospatial clustering
- Comparative service analysis
- Public narrative coding

## Scenario Taxonomy
- Medical events
- Pickup and dropoff issues
- Distress and comfort
- Navigation and routing
- General rider experience

## Data Sources
- Public reviews
- Public narratives
- Survey data
- Rider interviews
- Synthetic demonstration dataset used for workflow validation

## Policy Implications
- AV evaluation should extend beyond crash statistics.
- Rider experience affects adoption.
- Comfort, trust, and service design matter alongside safety.
- Public agencies need tools for monitoring public sentiment.

## Limitations
- Sampling bias
- Platform bias
- Sentiment classification uncertainty
- Synthetic demonstration data in prototype stage
